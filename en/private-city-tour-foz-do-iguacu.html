How a private city tour works

On a private city tour, the car and the driver are yours for the day. We pick you up at your hotel or apartment at the time you choose and follow the route we agreed with you beforehand on WhatsApp. If you want to stay longer somewhere, or skip a stop, just tell the driver.

The difference from a group tour is obvious from the start. Shared tours collect passengers from many hotels and give everyone the same amount of time at each stop. Here nobody waits for anybody. You leave when you are ready, eat where you like and head back when you have had enough.

The price is agreed before the tour, based on the route and the hours you need, so there are no surprises at the end of the day.

A suggested one-day route

This is the route we recommend most often for travellers with one full day in Foz. It catches each attraction at its best time and avoids crossing the city back and forth:

  • 8 am, Iguazu Falls (Brazilian side): arriving early means shorter queues for the park bus and soft morning light on the falls. Allow two to three hours.
  • 11 am, Parque das Aves: right across the road from the national park, so there is no extra drive. Around an hour and a half on the trail.
  • 1 pm, lunch: we suggest places on the way back or downtown, to suit your taste and budget.
  • 2:30 pm, Itaipu Dam: the panoramic tour runs along the dam by bus, with stops at viewpoints.
  • 5 pm, Triple Frontier Landmark: sunset over the Paraná River, with Argentina and Paraguay in view.

Prefer a slower pace? We drop the bird park or Itaipu and add the Buddhist Temple, which suits travellers looking for somewhere peaceful.

Places you can include

  • Iguazu Falls: the Brazilian side gives you the famous panoramic view and the Devil's Throat walkway.
  • Itaipu Dam: one of the largest hydroelectric plants in the world, with panoramic and technical tours.
  • Buddhist Temple: large gardens, Buddha statues and views over the Paraná River. A perfect break from a busy day.
  • Omar Ibn Al-Khattab Mosque: a symbol of Foz do Iguaçu's large Arab community, with architecture that surprises most visitors.
  • Triple Frontier Landmark: the meeting point of the Iguaçu and Paraná rivers, about 7 km from downtown.
  • Parque das Aves: walk-through aviaries with toucans, macaws and flamingos.

We plan the order by location. The falls and the bird park are on the road to the airport. Itaipu and the Buddhist Temple are north of the city, and the Triple Frontier Landmark is close to downtown.

Half day or full day

Half day (about four to five hours) works well if you arrive in the afternoon, have already seen the falls on another day, or just want the city highlights. A popular combination is the Buddhist Temple, the Mosque and the Triple Frontier Landmark at sunset.

Full day is ideal if you are short on time and want to see the falls and Itaipu on the same day. It means an early start, but saves you a second morning of driving.

Staying three days or more? It may be better to split things up: one day for the Argentine side of the falls, which needs four to six hours in the park, and another for the city tour in Brazil. Our private driver service can cover your whole stay.

Comfort for the whole family

The tour is done in an executive car with air conditioning: a sedan or SUV for up to four people, or a 7-seat vehicle for larger families, so nobody has to split into two cars.

Travelling with small children? Tell us when you book and we will arrange a child seat. For older travellers or anyone with reduced mobility, the driver drops off and picks up as close to each entrance as possible, which makes a real difference at large sites like Itaipu and the national park.

Shopping bags and backpacks stay in the car while you explore.

Tips for a great day

  • Buy your tickets for the falls, the bird park and Itaipu online, especially in July, December and January.
  • Bring a rain jacket or clothes that can get wet. Almost everyone gets soaked on the Devil's Throat walkway.
  • Sunscreen and water are essential in summer.
  • Check opening times before the tour. The bird park usually opens from 8:30 am to 5 pm, but hours change with the season.
  • For the Triple Frontier Landmark, arrive around 5 pm to catch the sunset and get a good spot for the evening show.
  • Most visitors only need a valid passport to enter Brazil, but rules depend on your nationality. Citizens of the United States, Canada and Australia, for example, need an e-visa for Brazil. Check the official requirements for your country before you travel.
